# 2025 Pedestrian Activity and Night-time Noise Statistical Results

Generated by `analysis/pedestrian_noise_statistical_analysis_2025.py`.

## Scope and analysis unit

The analysis uses one row per noise sensor at each buffer distance. The 500 m specification is primary. The 250 m and 1,000 m rows are sensitivity specifications and are not pooled as 33 independent observations. Pedestrian counts represent observed movements past counters, not unique people. Results are exploratory associations and do not establish causal effects.

The physical unit, sensor model, calibration, mounting height, noise-specific quality flags, and meaning of zero values could not be authoritatively confirmed. We therefore report recorded Noise values in dataset units.

The formal daily pedestrian metric is `daily_activity_analysis_value`, defined as the mean daily total among dates with all 24 hourly records. The observed-day field `avg_daily_pedestrian_count` remains descriptive only. The statistical models below use the pre-specified night-time hourly metric rather than either daily field.

## Primary 500 m association

Ten of the 11 noise sensors have at least one matched pedestrian counter within 500 m. For 2025 night-time median noise and mean night-time hourly pedestrian activity, Pearson r is 0.205 (p=0.571), while the primary Spearman rho is 0.248. The two-sided 99,999-draw permutation p-value is 0.494. With n=10, effect direction and stability matter more than threshold-based significance claims.

## Regression results

The simple 500 m OLS slope is 0.0167 dataset units per pedestrian count (95% CI -0.0483 to 0.0816; p=0.571; R-squared=0.042). The alternative activity-intensity slope is 0.000891 (p=0.303; R-squared=0.132). After adding bar capacity as a **lagged 2024 contextual baseline, not a 2025 measurement**, the pedestrian-mean coefficient is -0.0289 (p=0.444). This adjusted model has only 10 observations and must not be treated as confirmatory.

The largest Cook's distance in the primary model is 0.832 for ICTMicroclimate-10; the largest leverage is 0.414 for ICTMicroclimate-03; and the largest absolute externally studentized residual is 2.716 for ICTMicroclimate-10.

## Leave-one-sensor-out stability

Across the ten leave-one-out samples, Spearman rho ranges from 0.067 to 0.583. The simple OLS slope ranges from -0.0043 to 0.0460. These ranges show how strongly the result depends on individual monitored locations.

## Buffer sensitivity

For the mean night-time pedestrian metric, Spearman rho is 250 m: -0.168, 500 m: 0.248, 1000 m: 0.618. The corresponding simple-regression coefficients and confidence intervals are reported in `pedestrian_noise_models_2025.csv`. Differences across buffers reflect changing matched counter sets and should not be interpreted as separate independent samples.

## Reporting language

The analysis did not identify a stable simple association between the selected pedestrian measure and night-time recorded Noise values at these monitored locations.

The data do not support claims that pedestrian activity causes recorded Noise values, that results represent all of Melbourne, or that summed sensor means count unique pedestrians. The business-capacity fields are lagged 2024 contextual baselines, not contemporaneous 2025 measurements.
